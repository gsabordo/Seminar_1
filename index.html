Main takeaway

A good idea begins with the people who have the problem. Technology comes in only after the problem is clear.

Trend I noticed

There is a push to bring digital tools into sectors that are still mostly manual, with startups playing a big part in making this happen.

Government support

The DICT supports new ideas by raising awareness among students and young innovators.

Best practice

Start small by building a simple version of the idea, test it with real users, and improve it step by step instead of waiting for a perfect product.

Read the full narrative report

This webinar showed how an emerging technology idea can start from a very ordinary problem. I used to think of innovation as something that comes from big companies. The session showed that challenges in a field like agriculture can become tech opportunities when someone takes the time to understand them and design a useful solution.

I can think of improvements that fit community projects. A simple mobile or web app could help local farmers see current market prices or keep records of their crops and expenses. Even a basic online form and spreadsheet could be a first version that is later improved. As a student, I can try these ideas in my capstone or in a barangay project. This webinar encouraged me to look for problems around me and to see my IT skills as a way to solve them.
